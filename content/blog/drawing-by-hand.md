Every project I begin starts on paper, even when the deliverable will end up in a model.

## Slowness is the point

A pencil forces a decision at every line. There is no undo, so I think before I commit. The result is rarely neat, but it is almost always _considered_, and the drawing carries the intent that a hurried model can lose.

## Where the tools meet

I do not see hand drawing and software as rivals. Sketches settle the idea, and digital tools then test it: area schedules, sections, sun studies, coordination with engineers. Each is good at what the other is poor at.

- Sketch to **find** the idea.
- Model to **test** the idea.
- Draw again, by hand, to **explain** the idea to a client.

## A habit worth keeping

I keep a small sketchbook with me on site visits. Half the drawings are of details I never use, and that is fine: the looking is the training.
