Some buildings are remembered for their shape. The ones I return to are remembered for how they felt at four o'clock on an autumn afternoon.

## Daylight is a material

Concrete, timber and brick are specified in a schedule. Daylight is rarely given a line of its own, yet it does more to define a room than any of them. A deep reveal turns a flat wall into a clock, and a single high window can make a small room feel civic.

## Texture before colour

I tend to think about surface before palette. A board-marked concrete wall catches raking light and shows its making; a polished one hides it. Both can be right, but the choice should be deliberate.

- **Orientation** decides what light a room can ever have, long before anyone picks a finish.
- **Depth** (of a window, a soffit, a wall) is how a building holds shadow.
- **Restraint** with materials lets the light do the talking.

## What I take from this

Before I draw a plan, I try to describe the room in terms of light: where it enters, where it falls, and what it touches at the end of the day.
